## Parent Spec

`specs/0009-stock-aware-replenishment_2026-10-28_0914AM_UTC` (Part B, the slow-sales cut). This spec
replaces Part B. Parts A (fridge-stock cut) and C of 0009 are unchanged.

## Why

The menu import's "Slow-sales cut" (spec 0009 Part B) could only LOWER a fridge x category, and it
judged the pace by spreading the previous forecast evenly over the hours since the last delivery.
Two problems came up in the 16 Nov call:

1. **It cannot raise.** A fridge that sells faster than planned gets the same forecast again and
   empties early. Mathieu wants one control that goes up or down.
2. **Linear time is wrong for most categories.** Breakfast sells in the morning, cookies and snacks
   between 17:00 and 19:00. Checked at Tuesday noon, a breakfast category is almost "done" for the
   day while snacks have not started. A linear spread over-expects snacks at noon (and cuts them for
   no reason) and under-expects breakfast.

Agreed model (Ismail + Mathieu): expected sales come from past sales per FRIDGE x CATEGORY x WEEKDAY
x HOUR (category level, because products rotate). At import time:

    total expected by the next delivery = sold since the last delivery + forecast for the hours left
    pace = total expected by the next delivery / original forecast of that delivery
    new quantity = new forecast x pace   (minimum 50 %, may go up)

with a shelf-life check so a raise never sends units that expire before they can be sold.

## The model, step by step

For every fridge x category of the forecast being imported (delivery date `D`):

1. **Last delivery.** The category's newest restock ADDED event in the fridge within 14 days before
   `D` (unchanged from 0008/0009). Its local date is the previous delivery date `P`, its time is
   `delivered_at`.
2. **Original forecast `F`.** The saved forecast of `P` for this fridge x category: `gross_qty`
   (the demand before the fridge stock was deducted) when stored, else `forecast_qty`. No saved
   forecast for `P` -> no change.
3. **Forecast window.** `P`'s fill window: from `delivered_at` to local midnight of
   `P + days_to_fill` (the fridge's delivery config for `P`'s weekday). No config -> no change.
4. **Hourly profile.** Non-refunded `sales_events` of the last **8 weeks** (ending at local
   midnight of today), bucketed by Brussels local weekday and hour:
   * weight of one hour = (units sold on its weekday) x (units sold in its hour of day);
   * source: the fridge's own sales of the category when they total at least **56 units**
     (about one a day), else the category's sales in **all fridges** when those total at least
     56, else **linear** (every hour of a selling day weighs the same);
   * a day the fridge does not sell on (weekend for a fridge closed on weekends) weighs 0 whatever
     the source.
5. **Profile shares.** With `now` = the import time (never later than local midnight of `D`):
   * `W` = profile weight from `delivered_at` to the window end;
   * `E` = weight from `delivered_at` to `now` (elapsed);
   * `R` = weight from `now` to local midnight of `D` (remaining until the next delivery).
   Partial hours count pro rata.
6. **Noise guard.** `expected by now = F x E / W`. Below **5 units** the pace is noise: no change
   (kept from 0009).
7. **Pace.**
   * `forecast until next delivery = F x (E + R) / W` (equals `F` when the next delivery is the end
     of the window, the normal case);
   * `remaining = F x R / W`;
   * `pace = (sold since delivered_at + remaining) / forecast until next delivery`.
8. **Factor.** `factor = pace`, kept between **0.50** and **2.00**; rounded to 2 decimals.
   Factor 1.00 -> nothing is reported.
9. **Applied to the demand only.** The factor scales the new forecast's demand: its `gross_qty`
   when stored, else `forecast_qty`, rounded half up. The fridge-stock part of the target (the
   residual add-back of 0009) is not scaled, because stock already in the fridge is not demand.
   `new target = old target + round(demand x factor) - demand`, never below 0.
10. **Split.** The new target is split over the menu products as before (rating, optionally
    sold-first, then the fridge-stock cut).
11. **Shelf-life check (raises only).** When the target went UP, each product whose
    `shelf_life_days` is shorter than the new delivery's coverage window keeps the share it had
    without the raise. Coverage window = `coverage_end - D` in days, with `coverage_end` from
    `fridge_stock_service.coverage_end`: the next delivery, or sooner the first day the site is
    closed (a weekend for a fridge closed on weekends, or a bank holiday). Example: Thursday
    delivery, next delivery Monday, closed on weekends -> coverage end Saturday -> 2 days. The units
    a product gives up are not moved to other products. A product with no shelf life recorded, or a
    fridge with no delivery config on `D`'s weekday, is not held back.

## Worked examples

All numbers below are checked by tests in `backend/tests/test_weekly_menu.py`.

### Example 1: Mathieu's cookies (why the profile matters)

12 cookies delivered Monday 08:00, next delivery Wednesday. Checked Tuesday 12:00: 6 sold. Cookies
sell only between 17:00 and 19:00, the same every weekday.

| | Hourly profile | Linear |
|---|---|---|
| Window weight `W` | Mon 17-19 + Tue 17-19 = 4 hours | Mon 08:00 to Wed 00:00 = 40 h |
| Elapsed `E` | Mon 17-19 = 2 | 28 h |
| Remaining `R` | Tue 17-19 = 2 | 12 h |
| Expected by now | 12 x 2/4 = 6 | 12 x 28/40 = 8.4 |
| Remaining units | 12 x 2/4 = **6** | 12 x 12/40 = 3.6 |
| Pace | (6 + 6) / 12 = **1.00** | (6 + 3.6) / 12 = 0.80 |

The fridge is exactly on plan; the linear spread would have cut the next forecast by 20 %.

### Example 2: slower than planned (lower)

Desserts, 7-day window: delivered Wednesday 08:00, next delivery the following Wednesday (160 hours).
Original forecast 20. Checked Saturday 20:00 (84 hours elapsed, 76 left); no sales history yet, so
the profile is linear. 6 sold.

* remaining = 20 x 76/160 = 9.5
* pace = (6 + 9.5) / 20 = 0.775 -> factor **0.78**
* new forecast 15 -> 15 x 0.78 = 11.7 -> **12**, split 4 / 4 / 4 over ratings 66 / 70 / 75 %.

(The old slow-sales cut said: 10.5 expected by now, 6 sold, pace 0.57 -> cut to 9. The new rule
counts the 9.5 units still expected, so it reacts less on the first days and more as the window
runs out.)

### Example 3: faster than planned (raise) and the shelf-life check

Same as example 2 but 18 sold:

* pace = (18 + 9.5) / 20 = 1.375 -> factor **1.38**
* new forecast 15 -> 20.7 -> **21**, split 7 / 7 / 7 (without the raise: 5 / 5 / 5).
* Product 1 keeps 2 days; the delivery must last 7 (daily-open fridge, next delivery in 7 days).
  2 < 7, so product 1 stays at **5**. Result 5 / 7 / 7 = 19 units; the 2 units product 1 gave up
  are not moved to the others. The notice says "Not raised (7 -> 5): keeps 2 days, the delivery
  must last 7".

### Example 4: a fridge closed on weekends

Same fridge, closed on weekends, checked Sunday 08:00, 7 sold. Linear hours on selling days only:
window = Wed 16 h + Thu 24 + Fri 24 + Mon 24 + Tue 24 = 112 h; elapsed Wed 08:00 to Sun 08:00 = 64 h;
remaining Mon + Tue = 48 h.

* remaining = 20 x 48/112 = 8.57
* pace = (7 + 8.57) / 20 = 0.78

### Example 5: floor, ceiling and the noise guard

* Nothing sold after 84 of 160 hours: pace = 9.5/20 = 0.475 -> floor **0.50**, 15 -> 8.
* 40 sold: pace = 49.5/20 = 2.48 -> ceiling **2.00**, 15 -> 30.
* Original forecast 8: expected by now = 8 x 84/160 = 4.2 < 5 -> no change.

### Example 6: the fridge stock is not scaled

Gross demand 20, the forecast deducted 10 good units in the fridge (saved `forecast_qty` 10). At
half speed the demand is 10, and the fridge already holds 10: new target 10 + (10 - 20) = **0**.
Scaling the saved net 10 instead would have sent 5 units to a fridge that does not need them.

## Trade-offs and decisions

### Level from the forecast, shape from history

* **Chosen:** the profile only gives the SHAPE (how sales spread over hours); the LEVEL is the
  original forecast `F`. Remaining = `F x R / W`.
* Alternative A: remaining = the historical average units for those hours. Pros: independent of the
  forecast. Cons: a product rotation or a new client changes the level, and the history then
  disagrees with the forecast the pace is divided by (two different scales in one ratio).
* Alternative B: extrapolate the current pace (`sold / E x R`). Cons: this is the old linear cut in
  disguise and overreacts on the first day (2 sales in the first hours would project a huge week).
* Why chosen: it is Mathieu's formula read literally ("forecasted remaining until next delivery"),
  stays on one scale, reacts more as the window runs out, and with a flat profile it reduces to a
  simple time share that is easy to explain.

### Weekday x hour as two factors

* **Chosen:** weight = weekday total x hour-of-day total ("how busy is Tuesday" x "how busy is
  17:00").
* Alternative: a full 7 x 24 table per fridge x category. Cons: 168 cells from 8 weeks of a fridge
  that sells ~5 a day is mostly empty cells, so the shape would be noise.
* Risk: a weekday with its own hour pattern (a site that empties at Friday lunch) is smoothed into
  the week's average hours. Extension point: `HourlySalesProfile.hour_weight` is the one place to
  multiply in more factors (weather, or a full weekday x hour table once data is dense enough).

### Floor 50 %, ceiling 200 %, no dead band

* Floor 50 %: from the call. Ceiling 200 %: chosen here so one burst (an event at the client) can at
  most double a category; the shelf-life check and the fit-to-stock option limit it further.
* No dead band (a pace of 0.97 gives 0.97): the 5-unit noise guard already skips small categories,
  and rounding to whole units absorbs tiny changes. The old 70 % threshold is removed because the
  control now goes both ways.

### Shelf-life check

* **Chosen:** per product, only on a raise, compare `shelf_life_days` with the coverage window of
  the new delivery; a product that cannot last keeps its unraised share.
* Alternative: move the held units to longer-lasting products of the category. Cons: concentrates
  a demand guess onto fewer products and overstocks them.
* Unknown shelf life (about 218 products have none) does not block a raise: blocking would silently
  disable the feature for a third of the catalogue. Filling `shelf_life_days` makes the check
  stronger.
* A lowering never needs the check (fewer units cannot expire more).

### Profile thresholds

* 8 weeks: long enough for ~8 samples per weekday, short enough to follow the season.
* 56 units minimum (about one a day) before a profile is trusted; below that the category-wide
  profile is used, then linear.
* The profile ends at local midnight of today, so today's partial sales do not skew it.

## Related menu-import fixes in the same change

### Default-score banner listed hundreds of products

Root cause: when the imported scope had no chosen product (new week, or a category-scoped import
with nothing chosen), the rating suggestion split each forecast over **every active product of
the category** (623 active products on the testing DB, 237 of them unscored). The banner then
listed every unscored active product, 237 names, although only 10 of them got a unit, and every
suggested cell got 0 or 1 unit.

Fix:
* The banner lists only products on the imported menu that got units (share > 0) with the default
  score.
* A rating suggestion now starts from the **6 best-rated active products per category** (unscored
  products only fill up when a category has fewer than 6 scored). Testing DB, week 45 Wednesday:
  47 products suggested instead of 623, banner 0 instead of 237.

### Default score applied wrongly under sold-first

Scores are on one scale (0.49 to 0.90 on the testing DB, no zero or negative), and the category
mean for an unscored product is computed on that same scale, so the mean itself was right. The
wrong quantities came from **sold-first**: a new product sold nothing since the last delivery (it
was not in the fridge yet), so it only shared what the sold products left over. Testing DB
simulation (week 45 menu, one new product per category of 3+): rating split 45 units for the new
products, sold-first only 27 (-40 %); with real forecasts, where sales are close to the forecast,
the leftover is close to 0 and a new product gets 1 unit or none.

Fix: under sold-first a product with no score first takes its plain rating share of the full
target (the share it would get with sold-first off); sold-first then runs over the scored products
with what is left. Example (forecast 15, ratings 66 / 70 % and one new product, product 2 sold 11):
before 2 / 11 / 2, now 0 / 10 / **5**.

Also: a negative final score (possible with a loss-making margin) now weighs 0 instead of taking
units away from the others. A stored score of 0 counts as scored (weighs 0).

## API changes

`POST /api/v1/menus/import-from-forecast`

* Request: `slow_sales_cut` is replaced by `sales_speed` (bool, default false).
* Response: `sales_pace_cuts` is replaced by `sales_speed_adjustments`
  (`fridge_id, category_id, forecast_units, sold_units, remaining_units, pace, factor,
  profile_source`) and `shelf_life_holds` is added
  (`fridge_id, product_id, raised_qty, kept_qty, shelf_life_days, coverage_days`).
* `default_score_product_ids` now holds only products that got units.

No database migration.
